> **Premise correction:** **GM 1927 03 is not BIQS.** GM 1927 03 is the *Supplier Quality Statement of Requirements (SOR)*. BIQS lives in three other documents:
> - **GM 1927 30**: BIQS assessment / supplier self-assessment, the 30-element checklist.
> - **GM 1927 36**: BIQS training material.
> - **GM 1927 17**: SQ Processes & Measurement Procedure, which defines the level and metric definitions.

## Q1. Which documents define BIQS, which versions exist, and is BIQS still current as of Nov 2026?

### Takeaway
BIQS is defined by three documents:
- **GM 1927-30** (assessment). The latest revision found is **Rev 15.0, Feb 2020**. Earlier: v7.0, 2017-04-05.
- **GM 1927-36** (training). Reorganised into the "BIQS House" in **2018**.
- **GM 1927-17** (metrics and levels).

Since the GM IATF CSR revision effective **1 Jan 2023**, GM has renamed things:
- "BIQS Requirements" became **"GM Quality Performance Requirements (GM QPR)"**.
- "BIQS Level" became **"Sourceability Level"**.

The **January 2025 CSR** still requires an **annual GM 1927 30 "Quality Management System Gap Assessment"** for each manufacturing DUNS, done by self-assessment. So the 30-element checklist is still in use, but under a new label.

### Inferences
- As of Nov 2026, the right framing for training is:
  - "BIQS" survives as the **30-element GM 1927-30 QMS gap self-assessment**, done annually per DUNS.
  - The **level** (1–5) is now called the **Sourceability Level** and is driven by **GM QPR performance metrics**.
  - Slides should say "BIQS (GM 1927-30) elements" and should not cite "GM 1927 03" as the source.
- Rev 15.0 (Feb 2020) is the **most recent element list publicly visible**. A later revision may exist on GM SupplyPower; it is not public.

## Q2. What are the top-level categories (groupings), and which elements belong to each?

### Takeaway
The commonly assumed category names (Problem Solving / Manufacturing Process Control / Quality Systems Management / Supply Chain Management) **were not confirmed for BIQS**. The structure the sources do confirm is the 2018 **"BIQS House"** in GM 1927-36:
- a **Foundation** of Supply Chain Management, Maintenance and Training;
- then **Group A, B, C and D** element modules.

Only the Foundation and Group A were partly populated in the sources. Group C's module title is "Quality Focus and Inspection".

### Cited Findings
- **BIQS House foundation = Supply Chain Management, Maintenance, Training.** One guide calls them "a solid foundation in the BIQS House … essential for supporting the quality system's integrity and effectiveness" (a, mirrored GM 1927-36) — [Scribd: GM 1927-36 Introduction and Foundational Elements](https://www.scribd.com/document/481967216/GM-1927-36-Introduction-and-Foundational-Elements-of-BIQS)
- **The training modules** (a, mirrored) — [CourseHero: TRAINING BIQS 2019](https://www.coursehero.com/file/46085951/TRAINING-BIQS-2019pptx/); [Scribd: GM SQD Bulletin 2018-3](https://www.scribd.com/document/431576730/GM-SQD-Bulletin-1927-36-2018-3):
  1. GM 1927 36 Introduction and Foundational elements
  2. Group A elements
  3. Group B elements
  4. Group C elements
  5. Group D elements
- **Group A** contents, from a scraped slide index (a, mirrored; order and completeness uncertain) — [Scribd: GM 1927-36 Group A Elements](https://www.scribd.com/document/555139077/GM-1927-36-Group-a-Elements):
  - PFMEA
  - Process capability
  - Visual standards and controls
  - Error proofing and EP verification
  - Bypass management
  - Gauges calibration and MSA
  - Process control plan
  - Process change control
  - Layered audit
- **Group C**: the module is titled "BIQS Quality Focus and Inspection Guide"; its contents were not visible (a, title only) — [Scribd: GM 1927-36 Group C Elements](https://www.scribd.com/document/555138644/GM-1927-36-Group-C-Elements-BIQS)
- **Group D** exists as a separate module; contents not visible — [Scribd: GM 1927-36 Group D Elements](https://www.scribd.com/document/555138499/GM-1927-36-Group-D-Elements-BIQS)
- The older GM 1927-30 v7.0 (2017) and the SGM 2018 material list elements **as a flat sequence BIQS-1 … BIQS-30 with no category headings in the excerpts** (a/b) — [biqs.cn element list](https://www.biqs.cn/biqs-elements.html); [idoc.pub v7.0](https://idoc.pub/documents/gm-1927-30-biqs-audit-v70-2017-04-05-34wm3mgy0zl7)

### Inferences
- From the House order (Foundation, then Group A as risk/process-control design, then Group C as quality focus/inspection), the House reads bottom-up: **foundation, then prevent (process design and control), then detect/contain (inspection, quality checks), then respond/learn**.
- Group B probably holds the workplace/standardised-operation elements, and Group D the response/problem-solving elements. **This is inference only, not a sourced fact. Do not present it as GM's grouping.**
- For a deck, the safest approach is to present elements by their **classic BIQS-1…30 IDs**, which can be traced to GM 1927-30. The House grouping can be added as an organising visual, with a "per GM 1927-36 (2018)" caveat.

### Gaps
- I could not find the official element membership of Groups B, C and D. The Scribd pages could not be fetched.
- I found no evidence that BIQS uses the category names "Problem Solving / Manufacturing Process Control / Quality Systems Management / Supply Chain Management". Those names may come from QSB-era or third-party material.

There are **two numbering schemes**.

**(1) The classic GM 1927-30 IDs, BIQS-1 to BIQS-30**: v7.0 2017, the SGM 2018 training, and apparently Rev 15.0 2020. This scheme is reconstructed almost completely below.

**(2) The 2018 "BIQS House" training numbering in GM 1927-36.** It starts with 1 Maintenance, 2 Training, 3 Supply Chain Management, and only about 10 positions were recoverable.

BIQS-30 (Safety) is reported as **not counted in the BIQS level score**.

## Q4. What does each element require (key requirement statements), and why does GM require it (intent)?

### Takeaway
Element-level requirement detail was recoverable for about 18 of the 30 elements (mostly from SGM-derived training pages and GM CSR text). The rest are confirmed **by title only**.

**The recurring BIQS logic** (from the sourced requirements):
- Every check has a **defined frequency**: per shift, start of shift, monthly.
- Every check has a **reaction plan and escalation path**, usually into the **Fast Response meeting (BIQS-8)**.
- Every risk found is fed back into the **PFMEA, control plan and standardized work**.

## Q5. Which elements form "BIQS Level 1" (a critical subset), and how do BIQS / Sourceability Levels 1–5 work?

### Takeaway
**No source supports the idea of a "BIQS Level 1" element subset.** BIQS Levels 1–5 are a **supplier performance rating**, with 5 the best. They come from annual quality-performance metrics combined with the 30-element assessment. Suppliers must hold **Level 3, 4 or 5**; Levels 1–2 mean the GM QPR is not met. The closest thing to a "core subset" is GM training guidance to cascade **"at least the first 13 BIQS elements"** to key tier suppliers.

### Cited Findings
- BIQS (a) — [GM Scorecard quick reference, Aug 2020](https://www.iatfglobaloversight.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/GM-Scorecard-quick-reference-guide6Aug2020.pdf):
  - "establishes quality performance metrics the supplier must meet annually as well as a **30 point quality management assessment** of the supplier's manufacturing system aligned with GM and IATF standards".
  - Each element is scored **green / yellow / red** (b) — [ease.io 2018](https://www.ease.io/blog/what-auto-suppliers-need-to-know-about-new-gm-biqs/)
- The v7.0 colour scoring (a, mirrored) — [pdfcoffee v7.0](https://pdfcoffee.com/download/gm-1927-30-biqs-audit-v70-2017-04-05-pdf-free.html):
  - **Green** = system mature, well defined and followed as specified.
  - **Red** = system not evident or presenting significant risk to GM.
- **CSR level requirements** (a) — [IATF GM CSR Aug 2023](https://www.iatfglobaloversight.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/IATF-16949-GM-CSR-Aug-2023.pdf); [IATF GM CSR Jan 2025](https://www.iatfglobaloversight.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/IATF-16949-GM-Customer-Specific-Requirements-January-2025.pdf):
  - "Organizations shall achieve and maintain BIQS (now Sourceability) Level of **3, 4 or 5**".
  - Below Level 3, a **performance complaint** is submitted, which can lead to IATF certificate decertification.
  - "The GM system Sourceability Report will indicate a Sourceability Level of **1 or 2** for those organizations not meeting the GM Quality Performance Requirements."
  - "**Level 0** applies to sites with no production receipts nor any quality SPPS records in the last 12 months."
- **Sourcing rule:** if BIQS is **blank, 0 or RED**, sourcing requires a **Quad Report and SQ Leadership approval** (a) — [GM Scorecard quick reference, Aug 2020](https://www.iatfglobaloversight.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/GM-Scorecard-quick-reference-guide6Aug2020.pdf)
- **2020 tightening:** the criteria for **BIQS Levels 5 and 4** became more stringent, with **no allowed production part rejections (PRRs), field actions, disruptions or severity scores** (a, mirrored GM doc) — [Scribd: 2020 Quality Performance Requirements – BIQS Levels](https://www.scribd.com/document/466005233/2020-Quality-Performance-Requirements-BIQS-Levels-1-pdf)

- The metric and status definitions are in **GM 1927 17 SQ Processes and Measurement Procedure** (a, cited by CSR) — [IATF GM CSR Aug 2023](https://www.iatfglobaloversight.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/IATF-16949-GM-CSR-Aug-2023.pdf)

- **Level V** was a training target in the industry: an SDI workshop in Oct 2018 was titled "How to obtain & maintain a BIQS Level V" (b) — [events listing](https://discover.events.com/us/michigan/royal-oak/e/business/sdi-gm-biq-workshop-obtain-maintain-biqs-level-holiday-inn-269960484)

- Note: the "Level 1 = highest risk" in the 2015 BIQS deck refers to the **PFMEA severity/detection priority matrix**, not to the supplier BIQS level (b) — [idoc.pub BIQS training 2015](https://idoc.pub/documents/biqs-trainingmaterial-2015-2-jlk9v27zp745)

### Inferences
- For a "Level 5 대응 가이드", the message should be that **Level 5 is earned through performance outcomes**: zero PRRs, field actions, disruptions and severity scores over the 12-month window, plus a clean annual 1927-30 self-assessment. The elements are the means; **the level is measured on results**.
- **The first-13 subset as a proxy for the critical core** (inference):
  - Under the classic numbering, the first 13 elements run from BIQS-1 to BIQS-13: NC material, LPA, PFMEA, risk reduction, bypass, EP verification, gauges/MSA, Fast Response, problem solving, quality-focused checks, standardized work, process change control, and inspection gates.
  - This overlaps heavily with the legacy QSB strategies.
  - It is a reasonable proxy for the "critical core", but **it is not an official "Level 1 subset"**.
- **BIQS-30 Safety** is reportedly excluded from the level score. It may still be assessed.

### Gaps
- The exact GM 1927-17 formula and thresholds for each level (PRR counts, severity points, CS1/CS2, SPPS limits) were not found.
- The "12 months of quality history shown at Level 5" mentioned in one Korean summary could not be tied to a source. It is omitted.

## Q6. Were elements recently added or removed (e.g. cybersecurity, product safety, sustainability)?

### Takeaway
I found **no evidence** that new BIQS elements were added after 2020 for cybersecurity or sustainability. The documented changes are structural and terminological:
- **2017:** v7.0 wording changes to BIQS-3 and BIQS-4.
- **2018:** BIQS House reorder/renumber; SGM adopts BIQS.
- **2020:** Rev 15.0; stricter Level 4/5 criteria.
- **2023:** BIQS Requirements renamed GM QPR; BIQS Level renamed Sourceability Level.
- **2025:** Annual 1927-30 QMS gap self-assessment per DUNS; bypass-list customer approval plus PFMEA review; Oct 2025 changes to Sourceability-Level performance complaints and SPPS-based audit planning.

"Safety" (BIQS-30) has existed since at least the 2017–2018 lists and is not new.

### Cited Findings
- **v7.0 (2017-04-05)** revision notes (a, mirrored) — [idoc.pub v7.0](https://idoc.pub/documents/gm-1927-30-biqs-audit-v70-2017-04-05-34wm3mgy0zl7):
  - "modified PFMEA wording and structure (BIQS 3)"
  - "Add process of prioritizing high risk item to look for (BIQS 4)"
  - rework-part traceability under BIQS 1

- The trend from 2023 to 2025 is that **GM rates suppliers mainly on outcomes** (QPR metrics, SPPS) and keeps the 30-element checklist as an **annual self-assessed gap tool**. IATF certification bodies are told to use SPPS data when planning audits.
- For training slides, the 2025 bypass rule (customer-approved, PFMEA-reviewed alternatives) is the most concrete "new requirement" tied to a specific element (BIQS-5).
