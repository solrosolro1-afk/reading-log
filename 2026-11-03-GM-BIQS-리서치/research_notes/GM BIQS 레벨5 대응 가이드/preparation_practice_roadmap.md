## Q0 (prerequisite): What does "BIQS Level 5" actually mean in Nov 2026, and is there still a GM on-site BIQS audit?

### Takeaway
GM's official documents no longer use "BIQS Level". Since the CSR revision effective 1 Jan 2023, GM uses the **Sourceability Level**, driven by **GM Quality Performance Requirements (QPR, GM 1927 17)**. The level is computed from quality-performance history: SPPS/PRR counts and severity, field actions, disruptions, and IATF certification. It is not an on-site BIQS audit score. A forum report from 2019 says GM stopped running BIQS audits and dropped the audit score from the level. The practical consequence for a new supplier is that "Level 5" is mainly earned through a **clean 12-month quality history in GQTS**, and the BIQS element system is the means to get there. Korean industry and press still say "BIQS Level 5" in 2026.

### Inferences
- **Analysis:** For a new supplier, the target "BIQS Level 5 by Jul–Aug 2027" should be restated with GM Korea SQE/Purchasing as "Sourceability Level 5 on the GM Sourceability Report". The kick-off should confirm three things: (1) which QPR revision (GM 1927 17) applies; (2) how a DUNS with less than 12 months of receipts is scored, since Level 0 applies with no receipts or SPPS in 12 months; (3) whether GM Korea / GM International still runs an on-site BIQS or QMS assessment for new sites, or only the 1927 30 gap assessment.
- **Analysis:** If the level depends on a 12-month window of SPPS/field action/disruption history, the **SOP date and shipping volume** of the first GM parts largely set when Level 5 can appear. A supplier that starts shipping in early 2027 may show "Level 5" only if the QPR rules count a partial window as clean. This must be confirmed (see Gaps). The BIQS element system matters because it prevents the SPPS/PRR, disruption and CS events that pull the level down.
- **Analysis:** The term "BIQS" is still common in Korean practice and press. Training material should use "BIQS (현 Sourceability Level / GM 1927 17 QPR)" so staff recognize both.

### Gaps
- Current (2026) Level 1–5 thresholds in GM 1927 17 QPR are behind the SupplyPower login. Not found publicly.
- Not found: whether GM Korea (한국GM) or the GMI region still performs on-site BIQS assessments for new or launch suppliers in 2026, and whether any element-level score still feeds the level.
- Not found: the treatment of a new DUNS with fewer than 12 months of shipments (pro-rated vs. Level 0 vs. Level 5 once any receipt exists).

## Q1: Per-element practice — what "good" looks like on the shop floor, what evidence to have, and red flags

### Takeaway
Public GM material, mostly unofficial copies of GM 1927 30/1927 36 BIQS training and assessment forms from 2017–2020, gives a recurring set of concrete checks. Assessors and GM SQEs look for proof that the controls run on the floor every shift, not just a written procedure. Examples: an error-proofing verification log with red rabbits stored at point of use; reverse PFMEA findings fed back into PFMEA/CP/WI; reaction plans actually followed; every verification-station check backed by an upstream check; LPAs plus separate shift-level quality-focused checks; 3 closed problem-solving cases ready to show.

### Cited Findings
**Element structure / numbering**
- (a*, 2017) BIQS audit v7.0 lists elements including BIQS-7 Measurement System Analysis (with verification gage calibration), BIQS-8 Fast Response, BIQS-9 Team Problem Solving, BIQS-10 Quality Focused Checks, BIQS-11 Standardized Work, BIQS-12 Process Change Control, BIQS-13 Verification/Inspection gates, plus production trial runs and Andon. The extract is garbled, so exact numbering is unconfirmed. — [iDoc: GM 1927-30 BIQS Audit V7.0 2017-04-05](https://idoc.pub/documents/gm-1927-30-biqs-audit-v70-2017-04-05-34wm3mgy0zl7)
- (a*) BIQS-2 = Layered Process Audit. BIQS-29 relates to sub-tier supplier development and follow-up. GM 1927 36 training is split into element Groups A–D: Group A covers error proofing/PFMEA, Group D covers reverse PFMEA and contamination. — [GM 1927-36 Group A (Scribd)](https://www.scribd.com/document/555139077/GM-1927-36-Group-a-Elements); [Group C](https://www.scribd.com/document/555138644/GM-1927-36-Group-C-Elements-BIQS); [Group D](https://www.scribd.com/document/555138499/GM-1927-36-Group-D-Elements-BIQS); [GM 1927-36 training (PDFCoffee)](https://pdfcoffee.com/gm-1927-36-biqs-training-material-read-only-pdf-free.html)
- (a*, 2009) GM QSB (BIQS predecessor) strategy abbreviations: FR (Fast Response), NCP (Control of Non-conforming Product), WP/O (Workplace Organization), SWI/SOS (Standardized Work Instructions / Standardized Operation Sheet), OI/JES (Operator Instruction / Job Element Sheet), SOT (Standardized Operator Training), Risk (Risk Reduction). — [GM Quality System Basics Overview, rev March 2009](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/6019e43dcfad3c8c32417953/6019e43dcfad3c55b0417e6b_Supplier%20Audit%20-%20GM%20Quality%20System%20Basics%20Overview%20.pdf). QSB+ = 13 strategy directions including 快速反应 (fast response), LPA and 变更管理 (change management) — [Baidu Baike QSB (b)](https://baike.baidu.com/item/QSB/8536220)
- (b, Korean) A Korean quality-diagnosis paper describes BIQS as about 29 assessed items, including nonconforming identification, layered process audit, gauge calibration, standardized work and capability review. — [KAIS journal Vol20 No10 (군수품 품질수준 측정지표 연구)](https://www.kais99.org/jkais/journal/Vol20No10/vol20no10p14.pdf). *The count (29 vs 30) differs because BIQS-30 is reportedly not scored, see below.*
- (a*, 2020) The BIQS-30 element is not used to calculate the BIQS Level score. Suppliers should be ready to provide documentation for **3 closed problem-solving issues** showing the problem-solving process was followed. — [GM 1927 30 BIQS Supplier Self-Assessment rev 15.0 2020 (Scribd)](https://www.scribd.com/document/466005290/GM-1927-30-BIQS-Supplier-SelfAssessment-rev-15-0-2020-xlsx)

**Fast Response (BIQS-8)**
- (a*, 2006 QSB) GM material lists, as preparation for the fast-response meeting, identifying significant quality concerns from the **previous 24 hours**. — [GM QSB Workshop (Daum)](http://cfile281.uf.daum.net/original/165A14284A247F367BE2C0). *Attribution inside the aggregated search extract is probable, not certain.*
- (a*/b) A fast response can be triggered by a high-severity LPA nonconformance, an end-of-line test failure, a safety incident, a customer issue, etc. It requires verifying that suspect parts are contained and a root-cause investigation is started. — search extract from GM BIQS element material; candidates: [GM 1927-36 Group A](https://www.scribd.com/document/555139077/GM-1927-36-Group-a-Elements), [BIQS Presentation Aug 2017 (AnyFlip)](https://anyflip.com/elfcr/japv/basic/101-150). *Exact source not pinned.*
- (b) ease.io lists three "key" BIQS controls: LPA (tracking frequency, schedule, findings, corrective action); P-FMEA risk analysis applying severity/occurrence/detection tables consistently; and fast-response problem solving with **daily** monitoring linked to the LPA system. — [ease.io: Three Key GM BIQS Controls](https://www.ease.io/blog/three-key-gm-biqs-controls-every-organization-should-implement/)

**Layered Process Audit (BIQS-2) & Quality Focused Checks (BIQS-10)**
- (b) LPA is described as an ongoing chain of simple verification checks, done through observation, evaluation and conversations on the line, to confirm the process is performed correctly. GM's IATF CSR LPA requirement took effect 1 Nov 2017. — [ease.io: 3 Things to Know About GM IATF 16949 LPA Requirements](https://www.ease.io/blog/3-things-to-know-about-gm-iatf-16949-layered-process-audit-requirements/); [ease.io: Understanding New GM LPA Requirements](https://www.ease.io/blog/understanding-new-gm-layered-process-audit-requirements-in-iatf-16949/)
- (b) In BIQS-10 quality-focused checks, high-risk items from several areas get monitoring checks **each shift**. They can sit inside the LPA checklist, but per a GM-related interview, "LPAs on their own aren't enough". Suppliers that do sampling checks, or assume BIQS-2 covers it, get tripped up. — [ease.io: What Auto Suppliers Need to Know About New GM BIQS](https://www.ease.io/blog/what-auto-suppliers-need-to-know-about-new-gm-biqs/)
- (b, Tier-1 flow-down) Example Tier-1 requirement: a weekly internal LPA process to check adherence to standardized processes. — [SL America Supplier Requirements Manual](https://www.sl-america.com/wp-content/uploads/Supplier-Requirements-Manual-1.pdf)

**Verification Station / inspection gates (BIQS-13), Control of Non-conforming Product**
- (a*, 2017) BIQS-13 covers all inspection gates (in-process gates, final gates, GP12, CS1/CS2, verification stations). The intent is standardized inspection processes and current records. — [BIQS Presentation Aug 2017 (AnyFlip)](https://anyflip.com/elfcr/japv/basic/101-150)
- (a) "All items checked in the Verification Station (Final Inspection / CARE / GP12) must be included in a check at an upstream station." The final inspection is implemented per the local process. — GM CSR text per search extract; [GM CSR (ISO/TS) Oct 2016](https://www.iatfglobaloversight.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2016/12/GM-Customer-Specifics-Requirements-ISO-TS-16949-26Oct2016-1.pdf). *Not confirmed whether the same sentence is in the Oct 2025 CSR.*
- (a*) Material-handling failure modes (wrong parts, mixed parts, containment control) must be covered in the PFMEA. — [GM 1927 30 assessment guide (Scribd)](https://www.scribd.com/document/466005290/GM-1927-30-BIQS-Supplier-SelfAssessment-rev-15-0-2020-xlsx)

**Standardized Work (BIQS-11) / Training**
- (a) The GM CSR (2025) says quality checks shall be included in standardized work, and all standardized work shall be followed. — [GM CSR Oct 2025](https://www.iatfglobaloversight.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/IATF-16949-GM-Customer-Specific-Requirements-October-2025.pdf)
- (a*, 2017) Assessors ask to see the standardized work for each **bypass** process. — [BIQS Presentation Aug 2017 (AnyFlip)](https://anyflip.com/elfcr/japv/basic/51-100)
- (a) The IATF process-audit guidance says the auditor verifies operators' knowledge and application of procedures and work instructions. This is the basis for operator interviews. — [IATF Annex 2 Process Audit v1.1](https://www.iatfglobaloversight.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2016/12/Annex-2-Process-Audit.pdf)

**Risk Reduction: PFMEA, Reverse PFMEA, Error Proofing verification**
- (a*) A PFMEA risk analysis decides where error proofing is applied. Error-proofing masters ("Red Rabbit") must be clearly identified and stored in an assigned location near the point of use. "The supplier shall error proof to a level where it is not possible to ship defective products to GM." — [CG4338 GM 1927 03 Supplier Quality Statement of Requirements (dokumen.tips copy)](https://dokumen.tips/documents/cg4338-gm-1927-03-supplier-quality-statement-of-requirements.html); [GM 1927-36 Group A](https://www.scribd.com/document/555139077/GM-1927-36-Group-a-Elements)
- (a*) Error-proofing verification is a 100% check method, not sampling. All error-proofing devices are checked for function (failure or simulated failure) on a schedule (beginning of shift, start of operation, or start of test). — [GM 1927 30 assessment guide (Scribd)](https://www.scribd.com/document/466005290/GM-1927-30-BIQS-Supplier-SelfAssessment-rev-15-0-2020-xlsx)
- (a*/a) Reverse PFMEA is an on-station review of all PFMEA failure modes, verifying each has proper controls. Assessors check that findings are fed back into the Process Flow, PFMEA, Control Plan and Work Instructions. — [GM 1927-36 training (PDFCoffee)](https://pdfcoffee.com/gm-1927-36-biqs-training-material-read-only-pdf-free.html); [GM CSR May 2019](https://www.iatfglobaloversight.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2019/05/IATF-16949-GM-CSR-May-2019-_V4-1.pdf)
- (a*) Assessors look for evidence that the CP and PFMEA are reviewed and updated. — [GM 1927-36 training (PDFCoffee)](https://pdfcoffee.com/gm-1927-36-biqs-training-material-read-only-pdf-free.html); [GM 1927 30 self-assessment 2019 (PDFCoffee)](https://pdfcoffee.com/gm-1927-30-biqs-assessment-self-assessment-2019-pdf-free.html)

**Process control / SPC / capability / reaction plans; MSA & calibration (BIQS-7)**
- (a*, 2017) Assessors confirm that control-plan reaction plans are present, followed and effective; that sample sizes reflect occurrence and customer requirements; that capability studies are done annually; and that action plans against poor results are documented and followed. — [BIQS Presentation Aug 2017 (AnyFlip)](https://anyflip.com/elfcr/japv/basic/51-100)
- (a*, 2017) BIQS-7 combines MSA with verification-gage calibration. — [iDoc BIQS Audit V7.0](https://idoc.pub/documents/gm-1927-30-biqs-audit-v70-2017-04-05-34wm3mgy0zl7)

### Inferences
**Analysis — per-element implementation checklist.** This is synthesized from the cited checks above plus general GM/IATF practice. The "good looks like / evidence / red flags" columns are recommendations, not quoted GM text. Validate against the GM 1927 30 workbook obtained from SupplyPower or GM SQE.

| Element | What "good" looks like on the floor (analysis) | Evidence / records / visual boards to have ready (analysis) | Typical red flags (analysis, derived from cited checks) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fast Response (BIQS-8) | Daily stand-up at a fixed time and place (FR board) led by plant manager/quality head, covering the last 24h of internal and external concerns and GM issues. Each issue has an owner, containment status and a due date. Escalation to formal problem solving when not closed. | FR board (issue log, open/closed, GQTS/SPPS links), meeting attendance, closure evidence, link to LPA high-severity NCs and EOL failures | Board not updated daily; GM SPPS issues missing from the board; "closed" items with no verification of effectiveness |

| MSA & calibration (BIQS-7) | Gauges on the control plan have GR&R/attribute studies. Verification gauges and fixtures calibrated, with status visible on the gauge. | Calibration register and labels, MSA studies, gauge-change records | Expired calibration labels at stations; no MSA for attribute/visual checks |
| Process control / SPC / capability | SPC charts on special characteristics at the line, reaction plans followed when out of control, capability re-studied at least annually, action plans for low Cpk. | Control charts, OOC reaction log, annual capability summary, action plans | OOC points with no reaction entry; Cpk below target with no plan; sample size not matching the CP |
| Managing change (BIQS-12) | Change-control process covering product, process, material, tooling, sub-tier and temporary changes. Trial runs and first-off verification. GM notification/approval when required. | Change log, risk assessment per change, trial-run results, PPAP re-submission/notification evidence | Untracked temporary changes (alternate machine, deviation); sub-tier changes discovered late |
| Supply chain / sub-tier (BIQS-29) | Risk-based sub-tier audit plan (e.g., GM 1927 30-style or MAQMSR-based), sub-tier PPAP, flowed-down CSRs, incoming escapes treated with FR. | Sub-tier audit schedule and reports, gap-closure tracker, sub-tier performance scorecards | Audit plan exists but no follow-up closure; GM CSRs not flowed down |
| Contamination control | Cleanliness requirements identified (where applicable), contamination sources in the PFMEA, controls such as covered containers, cleaning schedules and FOD control. | Cleanliness specs and tests, cleaning schedule, container cleanliness checks | Open WIP containers; mixed dunnage; no link between contamination complaints and the PFMEA |
| Maintenance / TPM | Preventive/predictive maintenance on critical equipment (EP devices, test stands), spare-parts strategy, tool life management. | PM schedule and completion, breakdown/MTBF data, tool-life records | Overdue PM on critical machines; EP sensors replaced without re-validation |
| Traceability | Lot/serial traceability from raw material to GM shipment, with the ability to scope a suspect lot quickly. | Traceability drill results (time to scope), label/serial records | Cannot bound a suspect population quickly, which forces large containment and risks disruption |
| Part handling / packaging / labeling | Approved GM packaging and labels, FIFO, damage prevention, one-part-number-per-container rules. | Packaging specs, label verification checks, FIFO evidence | Mislabeling. Note: GM 1927 17 (GP-5) has a specific "Mislabeling" PRR category (see Q5). |
| Workplace organization (WP/O, 5S) | Defined locations, floor marking, visual controls, no obsolete parts or fixtures at the line. | 5S audit scores, shadow boards, red-tag area | Unidentified parts/fixtures; obsolete revisions at the line |

- **Analysis:** The evidence most likely to impress, or to fail, is the closed-loop chain on a single real issue. Example: a GM SPPS → FR board entry → containment → 8D/PPSR → PFMEA/CP/SW updated → LPA/QFC checklist updated → verification of no recurrence. Preparing 3 such complete cases matches the 1927 30 form's request for "3 closed problem-solving issues".

## Q2: Most frequent findings, and gaps between an IATF 16949 system and BIQS expectations

### Takeaway
No reliable public statistic of the "most common BIQS findings" was found. Recurring "watch-outs" can be read from what GM forms and consultants emphasize: sampling instead of per-shift quality-focused checks, LPA treated as the whole answer, reaction plans not followed, PFMEA/CP not updated, verification checks with no upstream control, missing bypass SW, and error-proofing verification gaps. The main IATF-vs-BIQS gap is that IATF certifies that a system exists, while BIQS (now QPR) judges daily shop-floor behavior and, above all, **GM-measured outcomes** (SPPS, disruptions, field actions).

### Cited Findings
- (b) ease.io flags BIQS-10 (quality-focused checks) as an area that "can easily trip up" suppliers that do sampling checks or assume LPA covers it. — [ease.io](https://www.ease.io/blog/what-auto-suppliers-need-to-know-about-new-gm-biqs/)
- (a*, 2017) GM assessor checklist items imply typical failure points: reaction plans present, followed and effective; sample sizes; annual capability; action plans for poor capability; SW for bypass processes. — [BIQS Presentation Aug 2017 pp. 51–100](https://anyflip.com/elfcr/japv/basic/51-100)
- (a*) PFMEA/CP review and update evidence, and reverse-PFMEA findings feeding back into PFD/PFMEA/CP/WI, are explicit audit checks. — [GM 1927-36 training](https://pdfcoffee.com/gm-1927-36-biqs-training-material-read-only-pdf-free.html); [GM CSR May 2019](https://www.iatfglobaloversight.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2019/05/IATF-16949-GM-CSR-May-2019-_V4-1.pdf)
- (a) The GM CSR adds requirements beyond base IATF 16949 that mirror BIQS: quality checks in standardized work, adherence to SW (2025), and LPA (from 2017). — [GM CSR Oct 2025](https://www.iatfglobaloversight.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/IATF-16949-GM-Customer-Specific-Requirements-October-2025.pdf); [ease.io on GM LPA CSR](https://www.ease.io/blog/3-things-to-know-about-gm-iatf-16949-layered-process-audit-requirements/)

- (c) Loss of Level 3 (">6 SPPS in 6 months") led to an IATF **Major** NC at surveillance. The poster struggled to define a "systemic" CA. — [Elsmar thread](https://elsmar.com/elsmarqualityforum/threads/received-a-major-finding-during-iatf-surveillance-audit-for-loss-of-biqs-level-3-more-than-6-spps-in-6-months-how-should-we-address-systemic-ca.77943/)
- (b, non-GM, general) Generic supplier audit findings cluster in document control, training management, CAPA, change control and supplier management. These are life-science/ISO sources, so transferability to GM is limited. — [SimplerQMS: audit findings](https://simplerqms.com/audit-findings/)

### Inferences
- **Analysis — typical IATF→BIQS gaps for an IATF-certified Korean supplier:**
  1. **Frequency and depth:** IATF manufacturing process audits (9.2.2.3) are periodic. BIQS expects **daily** fast response, **per-shift** QFC/EP verification, and multi-layer LPA. Records must show that rhythm.
  2. **Verification station logic:** IATF requires final inspection but not the GM rule that every verification-station check has an upstream counterpart, nor the feedback loop to the source station.
  3. **Reverse PFMEA on station:** IATF requires PFMEA maintenance. BIQS expects documented on-station reverse PFMEA walk-throughs feeding back into documents.
  4. **Red rabbit discipline:** IATF 16949 already requires error-proofing and its testing (clause 10.2.4). BIQS adds the expectation of identified masters stored at point of use and EP strong enough that defects "cannot be shipped".
  5. **Bypass/temporary process SW** and reaction-plan execution evidence. This links to IATF 8.5.6.1.1 temporary process control changes, but BIQS checks it at the station.
  6. **Customer-measured outcome:** an IATF certificate does not protect the Sourceability Level. One SPPS cluster, a disruption or a CS event can drop the level regardless of system maturity.
  7. **GM-specific systems:** GQTS/SPPS responsiveness, EPC (GM 1927 28) instead of generic safe launch, and GM PPAP/run-at-rate specifics.
- **Analysis — likely high-frequency findings at a new Korean plant (hypothesis to validate in mock audits):** LPA "pencil-whipping" with 100% OK; FR boards not covering GM SPPS; SW/JES not matching what operators do, especially for 파견/temporary workers; PFMEA not updated after complaints; missing or damaged red rabbits; expired gauge labels; NC parts not segregated at stations; no traceability drill evidence.

### Gaps
- No consultant or GM-published Pareto of BIQS findings was found (English, Korean or Chinese). Korean "BIQS 지적사항 / 심사 후기" content (네이버 블로그/카페) did not show up in search results. It may be behind Naver login/cafe walls.

## Q3: How the GM on-site assessment is (or was) conducted, and how to prepare (self-assessment, mock assessment, internal auditors, gap tracking)

### Takeaway

### Inferences
- **Analysis — what to expect if GM Korea SQE (or a GM-appointed assessor) does visit**, modeled on GM forms and IATF process-audit practice, not on a published GM agenda:
  - Opening (~30 min): plant overview, GM part list, quality performance (SPPS, disruptions, CS history), FR board.
  - Shop-floor walk (most of the day): follow product flow from receiving to shipping. At selected stations, compare SW to actual work, ask operators about key points and reaction plans, watch an EP verification with the red rabbit, look at SPC/reaction logs, verification-station checks against upstream checks, NC segregation, gauge calibration labels, bypass SW.
  - Document sampling: 3 closed problem-solving cases; PFMEA/CP revision history against complaints; LPA records and NC closure; change log; sub-tier audit plan; training matrix.
  - Closing: findings per element, action plan with owners and dates. Follow-up in GM systems or by email. *GQTS hosts SPPS and EPC records. Whether BIQS action plans still go into GQTS was not confirmed.*
- **Analysis — preparation practice:**
  1. Obtain the current GM 1927 30 workbook and GM 1927 17 QPR from SupplyPower (or GM Korea SQE). Use them as the self-assessment baseline, scoring each element strictly ("no evidence = not met").
  2. Train 4–6 internal assessors, cross-departmental, ideally IATF internal auditors upgraded with GM CSR and 1927 30 content. Run the self-assessment as a **floor-first** audit: 70%+ of the time at stations, interviews in Korean with the operator, evidence photographed.
  3. Keep a single gap-closure tracker (element, finding, root cause, action, owner, due date, evidence link, verification date, status), reviewed weekly by the plant manager.
  4. Run **2 mock assessments**: Mock 1 about 8–10 weeks after deployment (diagnostic), Mock 2 about 4–6 weeks before the target (readiness), ideally with an external GM-experienced assessor or a sister-plant auditor.
  5. Prepare the evidence pack: 3 closed PS cases, 3–6 months of FR/LPA/QFC/EP-verification records, PFMEA/CP revision history, traceability drill, sub-tier audit closure.

## Q5: Quality-performance side for top levels — zero PRR/SPPS, zero CS, launch (PPAP, GP-12 → EPC exit), GM problem-solving formats

### Takeaway
GM renamed key launch and complaint tools: **GP-12 → Early Production Containment (EPC, GM 1927 28)**, and **PRR → SPPS record** (Supplier Practical Problem Solving) in GQTS. EPC runs 100% for a default 90 days after SORP, and exit needs objective evidence through a GM 1927 39 exit request. GM 1927 17 requires containment that is extraordinary and visible, and problem solving that is formal, data-driven and documented. Controlled Shipping 1/2 is the escalation path, and CS2 must be reported to the registrar within 5 working days. No GM-official "Red X" requirement was found.

### Cited Findings
- (a) The GM CSR revision history says GP-12 was replaced with Early Production Containment (EPC). — search extract of [GM CSR Jan 2025](https://www.iatfglobaloversight.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/IATF-16949-GM-Customer-Specific-Requirements-January-2025.pdf)
- (a*, June 2020 copy) GM 1927 28 EPC: EPC stays in place until **90 calendar days after SORP**, with mandatory EPC inspection of 100% of parts. The supplier becomes eligible to exit after validating the effectiveness of the PCP and meeting the exit criteria. The exit request uses the **GM 1927 39** exit request letter and needs supporting documentation. The customer verifies, and "decision to exit shall only be based on objective evidence". If nonconformances are found, containment continues; one GM-derived copy says at least two weeks after implementing corrective actions. The revision history also notes PRR was replaced by the "Supplier Practical Problem Solving (SPPS) record". — [GM 1927 28 EPC June 2020 (Henniges)](http://www.hennigesautomotive.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/GM-1927-28-Early-Production-Containment-EPC.pdf); [Scribd GM 1927-28](https://www.scribd.com/document/737072453/GM-1927-28-Early-Production-Containment-EPC)
- (b, Tier-1 flow-down) Nexteer: the default EPC is 90 regular production days unless instructed otherwise, and the last 30 days must run on the regular control plan. — [Nexteer EPC training 2014](https://www.nexteer.com/wp-content/uploads/EPC_training-2014-07-21.pdf). Nemak: if issues are found during EPC, exit typically requires the root cause identified, an irreversible CA implemented, and a sustained defect-free period. — [Nemak SQM 2023](https://www.nemak.com/media/2636/nemak-supplier-quality-manual_21082023_final-version.pdf)
- (a, GM CSR) The problem-solving clause 10.2.3 was revised to require timely closure of corrective actions, including exit criteria that verify the true root cause and CA effectiveness. — search extract of [GM CSR Jan 2025](https://www.iatfglobaloversight.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/IATF-16949-GM-Customer-Specific-Requirements-January-2025.pdf); [GM Scorecard Quick Ref Aug 2020](https://www.iatfglobaloversight.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/GM-Scorecard-quick-reference-guide6Aug2020.pdf)
- (a*, 2015 GP-5) GM 1927 17: containment must be "extraordinary (nonstandard production process), visible". Problem solving "must be formal, data driven and documented". The SPPS record moves from "containing" to "approving" after containment, completion of the Problem Solving Questionnaire and root-cause identification. GM may delete a PRR in GQTS after root-cause analysis (item 8-3). A first offense for a DUNS in 6 months is classified as a level E "Mislabeling" PRR. — [GM 1927-17 GP-5 (PDFCoffee)](https://pdfcoffee.com/gm-1927-17-sq-processes-and-measurements-procedure-gp-5-pdf-free.html); [GM 1927-17 Rev 9/10 (Scribd)](https://www.scribd.com/document/481967061/GM-1927-17-Supplier-Quality-Processes-and-Measurements-Procedure-Rev-9-10). *2015-era. May be superseded.*
- (a*) GM 1927-48 is the **PPSR** (Practical Problem Solving Report) form used with SPPS. — [Scribd GM 1927-48 PPSR Form SPPS](https://www.scribd.com/document/451169069/GM-1927-48-PPSR-Form-SPPS-8219-update-3)
- (a) For the PPM metric, a supplier is impacted when a quality PRR with a quantity discrepancy is written and there are receipts for that part in the previous 12 months. — search extract, [GM Scorecard Aug 2020](https://www.iatfglobaloversight.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/GM-Scorecard-screen-images_6Aug2020.pdf)
- (a) Controlled Shipping Level 2: the organization must notify its IATF certification body in writing within **5 working days**. Parts stay under CS until CAs are verified. — search extract of [GM CSR Jan 2025](https://www.iatfglobaloversight.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/IATF-16949-GM-Customer-Specific-Requirements-January-2025.pdf)
- (b, Tier-1 flow-down using GM model) CS1 = inspection by the supplier's own employees at the supplier's location. CS2 adds inspection by an impartial third party. CS may be issued when a lack of process control has let nonconforming parts reach the customer plant. Exit requires zero nonconformances at the customer during CS. — [Nexteer Supplier Containment Instruction Dec 2022](https://www.nexteer.com/wp-content/uploads/Supplier_Containment-1.pdf)
- (b, non-GM) A MAHLE 8D manual uses 24 hours for D1–D3 containment communication. This is MAHLE's rule, not GM's. — [MAHLE 8D manual](https://www.mahle.com/media/global/purchasing/supplier-portal/8d-e-learning/manual_8d-process_en-ex.pdf). 8D is the default complaint format across Ford/GM/Stellantis Tier-1s per a 2026 guide — [5xwhys 8D guide (b)](https://5xwhys.com/articles/8d/guide/)
- (c, Korean) DN Automotive frames its BIQS system around "SPPS, Field Action zero". — [DN AUTOMOTIVE](https://www.dnautomotive.com/sub/business/quality_vms.asp)

### Inferences
- **Analysis — keeping zero SPPS/CS/disruption in the launch year:**
  - Treat EPC as a **learning system**, not just a firewall. Every EPC hit gets an FR entry and a root-cause review. Plan the EPC exit package (GM 1927 39 letter, defect-free data, PCP validation) from day one. Avoid "exit by calendar".
  - Mislabeling and mixed parts are cheap SPPS sources. Shipping-label verification (scan-to-scan) and one-part-per-container rules give fast payback.
  - Hold disruption risk at sub-tiers: sub-tier EPC/safe launch, a capacity check (run-at-rate) and change freeze during the clean-history window.
  - Answer GM SPPS with the GM format (PPSR/SPPS questionnaire in GQTS), backed by internal 8D/5-Why. Close with exit criteria (verified root cause and CA effectiveness) and update PFMEA/CP/SW/LPA every time.
- **Analysis:** "Red X" (Shainin) is a useful internal diagnostic tool for chronic issues, but I found **no evidence** that GM requires it in SPPS responses.

### Gaps
- Current GM 1927 17 response-time requirements (e.g., hours to containment, days to root cause in GQTS) were not found in public sources. The 2015 GP-5 copy lacked specific timings in the extracts.
- Current GM PPAP specifics (GM supplement to AIAG PPAP, run-at-rate) were not researched in depth here and may be covered by other research notes.
- No evidence found for any GM "Red X" requirement.
